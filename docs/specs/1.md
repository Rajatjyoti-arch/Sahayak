## 0. How to read this brief

This brief separates decisions from hypotheses. Do not turn a hypothesis into a fact when copying text into a business plan or presentation.

- **LOCKED DIRECTION** — current agreed product/strategy direction; may change only if new evidence justifies it.
- **PROVISIONAL ASSUMPTION** — useful for planning; must be tested or confirmed.
- **OPEN DECISION** — not settled; do not present as final.
- **ACCEPTANCE TEST** — evidence required before a capability is described as working.

## 1. Executive decision

The venture will build an **offline-first governance software platform** around a narrow first product: **Scheme Reconciliation Assistant**, a desktop workflow that reconciles two administrative spreadsheets, surfaces questionable records, keeps the original files unchanged, supports human adjudication, and produces a structured workbook and verifiable audit record.

The product is not an AI decision-maker. Its core principle is:

> **AI suggests. Deterministic code calculates. The officer decides. Every material action is traceable.**

The strategic sequence is:

1. Demonstrate a reliable reconciliation workflow using synthetic data.
2. Validate it with intended users and authorised institutional partners.
3. Prove measurable improvement on a controlled pilot.
4. Extend the common platform to additional administrative workflows only after each new workflow is independently tested and accepted.

The product vision is intentionally broader than the MVP. The first demo must not depend on features that are still speculative, such as long-form note drafting or handwritten document OCR.

## 2. Three levels of scope

### 2.1 Vision — 3 to 5 years

**Vision statement:** Make reliable, privacy-conscious AI assistance practical for public administration, including offices where connectivity, hardware and technical capacity are constrained.

The long-term platform may help authorised public-sector staff process fragmented administrative records into structured, reviewable and auditable work products. Potential future workflow areas include document extraction, petition triage, circular search, archival retrieval and administrative drafting.

These are roadmap possibilities, not commitments that all modules will be delivered or accepted by government.

### 2.2 Product platform — approximately 6 to 24 months, subject to gates

The reusable platform is intended to contain:

- A local desktop interface and controlled file-ingestion layer.
- Deterministic, testable workflow modules.
- Optional local-model assistance for bounded semantic tasks.
- A human review and exception-adjudication interface.
- Structured export and provenance/audit facilities.
- Configuration and update mechanisms suitable for offline environments.

The platform should remain useful when the local language model is unavailable, too slow, or produces an invalid suggestion. The AI is an assistant to the workflow engine, not a prerequisite for core arithmetic or record conservation.

### 2.3 First MVP — Scheme Reconciliation Assistant

**MVP promise:** Import two sanction/disbursement spreadsheets, reconcile records conservatively, identify exceptions, allow a human to review ambiguous candidates, and export an auditable result without sending source data to a cloud AI service.

The MVP has one hero workflow, one core buyer/user hypothesis, and one proof story. All additional features must be justified by evidence that they are necessary for this workflow.

## 3. Problem hypothesis and target user

### 3.1 Problem hypothesis

Offices may need to compare records received from different systems or in different spreadsheet formats. Even when spreadsheet formulas or scripts can perform the comparison, the complete process may remain dependent on staff maintaining formulas, interpreting exceptions, handling inconsistent headers and retaining defensible records of changes and approvals.

The product hypothesis is not that Excel or Python cannot reconcile data. It is that a packaged, guided workflow can reduce repeated setup and operational error while improving transparency and reviewability for non-programmer users.

### 3.2 Initial user/buyer hypothesis

- **Daily user:** dealing assistant, clerk or authorised administrative staff member who prepares or checks reconciliation statements.
- **Reviewer:** head assistant, section officer or other authorised supervisor.
- **Potential economic buyer:** the relevant department/office through its lawful procurement process.
- **Potential route to first pilot:** university/incubator introduction to an authorised administrative partner, followed by written permission and a bounded pilot design.

These roles and the exact procurement unit must be validated in interviews. Do not assume every office has the same authority, budget, workload or staffing structure.

### 3.3 Questions to validate with users

1. Which two files are actually compared, and who generates each one?
2. How many records are typical, and how frequently is reconciliation performed?
3. What are the common error types and the current resolution path?
4. Which identifiers are consistently available and lawfully usable?
5. What is the baseline task time, and how is it measured?
6. What output format and approval record would the supervisor accept?
7. What are the office's data-handling, device, installation and procurement requirements?
8. Who would be authorised to approve a pilot and later purchase software?

Do not claim a 15–22-hour weekly workload, months of benefit delays, or a 90% time saving unless independently measured or supported by a credible source.

## 4. MVP feature boundary

### 4.1 Required for first MVP

| Capability | Requirement | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| File ingestion | Import two CSV/XLSX files; originals remain unchanged | Input hashes before/after match; test with malformed files |
| Schema mapping | Known aliases are handled deterministically; unknown headers may receive local-model suggestions | User sees and approves mapping; invalid suggestions can be rejected |
| Normalization | Trim whitespace and normalize safe representations without destroying raw source values | Raw and normalized values can be inspected side by side |
| Exact matching | Match records using validated identifiers and explicit rules | Known test cases yield expected pairs |
| Fuzzy candidate search | Generate candidates from multiple identity signals; never auto-approve fuzzy pairs | Review queue contains rationale and conflicting fields |
| Duplicate handling | Detect possible duplicates and apply explicit canonical/excluded-row rules | Duplicate rows remain visible in report and are never silently dropped |
| Financial calculation | Parse amounts into integer paise or exact decimal form at ingestion; calculate without binary-float arithmetic | Unit tests cover inputs, malformed values and reconciliation totals |
| Review workflow | Approve, reject or flag for field inquiry; record each decision | Reviewer action appears in event history and export |
| Export | New workbook with clearly separated matched, unmatched, pending, duplicate and invalid records, plus totals | Workbook cross-checks against application summary |
| Audit trail | Record run ID, source/output hashes, sequence, event type, operator identity reference and timestamps | Verification utility detects altered, reordered or missing events within the anchored chain |
| Offline operation | Core workflow works with networking disabled | Repeatable offline demo and separately documented network-behaviour test |

### 4.2 Stretch features only if the core is stable

- Optional local-LLM draft of a one-page administrative exception note using verified aggregate data.
- Additional configurable header aliases or department schemas.
- Additional export templates.

A stretch feature must not delay or destabilize the core reconciliation demo. The first MVP remains useful if the LLM is disabled.

### 4.3 Explicit non-goals

The MVP will not:

- Approve beneficiary eligibility or authorise or initiate payments.
- Claim to verify a person's identity or validate an official government identity record.
- Automatically merge fuzzy matches without an explicit human decision.
- Use actual Aadhaar data for the hackathon demo.
- Depend on a cloud API, internet lookup, telemetry or remote inference for core functionality.
- Claim universal legal compliance, perfect accuracy, zero hallucinations or zero security risk.
- Include handwriting recognition, multilingual OCR, circular search, full records-management functionality or all governance workflows as MVP commitments.

## 6. Matching policy: separate identity linkage from financial reconciliation

### 6.1 Two separate questions

The engine must keep the following separate:

1. **Record linkage:** Do these two rows plausibly describe the same underlying case or person/transaction?
2. **Financial reconciliation:** Once linked, do the relevant sanctioned and disbursed amounts agree, and are there other discrepancies?

**An amount mismatch must not automatically prevent the engine from linking otherwise corroborated records.** If the amount is made a mandatory equality condition for candidate creation, the system will fail to detect the very disbursement discrepancies it is meant to report.

### 6.2 Matching stages

1. **Schema validation:** Map source fields to canonical fields, preserving raw values.
2. **Reliable key match:** Use identifiers whose meaning is validated and which are shared across the files, if available.
3. **Candidate generation:** Use blocking keys and multiple identity signals to reduce comparisons. A masked account suffix is not unique and must never be sufficient by itself.
4. **Candidate scoring:** Consider the availability and agreement of name, parentage, masked account suffix, IFSC, location/other permitted fields and any reliable identifiers. Missing fields must not be treated as agreement.
5. **Amount comparison:** Compare sanctioned and disbursed amounts after a record pair is proposed; treat mismatch as an exception, not as an automatic identity veto.
6. **Human adjudication:** Present source values, rationale and conflicting fields. A fuzzy candidate remains pending until an authorised reviewer confirms or rejects it.
7. **One-to-one enforcement:** Unless a documented workflow explicitly allows otherwise, one source row cannot be paired with multiple rows from the other file. Potential many-to-one/many-to-many conflicts must go to review.

### 6.3 Candidate thresholds

A name-similarity threshold such as `0.82` may be used as an initial **experiment parameter**, not a universal rule or proof of identity. The score depends on the selected similarity algorithm, input normalization, script, language and data quality.

Thresholds should be calibrated on a labelled synthetic test set and then re-evaluated with authorised representative data before any real deployment. Report precision and false-match rates. Do not tune only for a high match count.

### 6.4 Queue actions

- **Approve as match:** creates a confirmed pair, with reviewer/action/time recorded.
- **Reject / keep separate:** records rejection and keeps both records unlinked.
- **Flag for field inquiry:** preserves the unresolved case and records the reason.

Approval means only that the reviewer accepted the record association for reconciliation. It does not mean the beneficiary is eligible, the data is authentic, or payment is authorised.

## 7. Row-conservation and financial invariants

### 7.1 Exactly one primary disposition per row

Each imported source row must be assigned exactly one primary disposition. Secondary warning flags may coexist with any disposition, but they do not count as additional rows.

Permitted primary dispositions:

- `MATCHED`
- `UNMATCHED`
- `DUPLICATE_EXCLUDED`
- `INVALID_FORMAT`
- `PENDING_REVIEW`

The processing order should be explicit and deterministic. A proposed priority is:

1. `INVALID_FORMAT` if required fields cannot be safely interpreted.
2. `DUPLICATE_EXCLUDED` for a non-canonical row excluded under the declared duplicate policy.
3. `PENDING_REVIEW` for an unresolved plausible candidate.
4. `MATCHED` for a unique exact pair or an explicitly reviewer-confirmed candidate.
5. `UNMATCHED` for all other usable rows.

Secondary flags such as `POSSIBLE_DUPLICATE`, `AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `INVALID_IFSC_FORMAT` or `NAME_CONFLICT` remain visible even when a row has one primary disposition. The status priority and duplicate policy must be tested so no row disappears.

For File A (sanction list), define:

\[
N_A = M + U_A + D_A + I_A + P_A
\]

For File B (disbursement report), define:

\[
N_B = M + U_B + D_B + I_B + P_B
\]

where `M` is the number of confirmed one-to-one pairs; `U` is unmatched rows; `D` is duplicate-excluded rows; `I` is invalid rows; and `P` is pending-review rows. Each confirmed pair contains one row from each file and is counted once in each file's partition. The dashboard may display `M` as pair count, but it must not add the two files' `M` counts and describe that as matched-pair count.

### 7.2 Financial rules

- Currency inputs are parsed at the boundary and represented as integer paise for calculations.
- No arithmetic total is calculated by the LLM.
- No invalid amount is silently coerced to zero.
- Rounding or excess decimal places are never silently truncated.
- Invalid amounts make the relevant row `INVALID_FORMAT` or a clearly defined amount-validation error and remain in the export.
- Record linkage and amount agreement remain separate.

For each confirmed pair `i`:

\[
\Delta_i = S_i - D_i
\]

where `S_i` is the sanctioned amount in paise and `D_i` is the disbursed amount in paise. The matched-pair variance is:

\[
\Delta_{matched}=\sum_{i \in confirmed\ pairs}(S_i-D_i)
\]

Unmatched sanction amounts, unmatched disbursement amounts, pending-review amounts and excluded/invalid amounts must be reported separately. Never label matched-pair variance as the complete explanation of all scheme-wide discrepancies when unresolved or unpaired records remain.

A useful summary bridge, calculated only over the clearly defined included/canonical valid rows, is:

\[
\sum S - \sum D = \Delta_{matched} + S_{unmatched} + S_{pending} - D_{unmatched} - D_{pending}
\]

The precise inclusion policy for duplicate-excluded and invalid records must be documented in the workbook so totals cannot double-count duplicate rows or hide invalid data.

### 7.3 Strict paise parsing

For text-formatted amounts, accept only declared formats such as `5000`, `5000.50`, `₹ 5,000.50` and supported Indian comma grouping. Reject empty strings, multiple decimal points, negative values unless explicitly supported by the product rules, malformed comma grouping and more than two fractional digits. Do not strip arbitrary characters from an input and then assume the remainder is valid. Do not silently convert `5000.999` to `5000.99`.

Reference implementation for a strict text input:

```python
import re

_AMOUNT_RE = re.compile(
    r"^(?:\d+|\d{1,3}(?:,\d{3})+|\d{1,2}(?:,\d{2})*,\d{3})"
    r"(?:\.\d{1,2})?$"
)


def parse_to_paise(amount_text: str) -> int:
    """Parse a non-negative currency string into integer paise.

    Accepted examples: '5000', '5000.50', '₹ 5,000.50', '1,00,000.00'.
    Malformed or over-precision inputs are rejected rather than rounded/truncated.
    """
    if not isinstance(amount_text, str):
        raise TypeError("Expected a currency string; handle numeric spreadsheet cells explicitly")

    cleaned = amount_text.strip()
    cleaned = re.sub(r"^(?:₹|INR|Rs\.?)\s*", "", cleaned, flags=re.IGNORECASE)
    cleaned = re.sub(r"\s+", "", cleaned)

    if not cleaned or not _AMOUNT_RE.fullmatch(cleaned):
        raise ValueError(f"Invalid currency amount: {amount_text!r}")

    normalized = cleaned.replace(",", "")
    rupees, dot, fractional = normalized.partition(".")
    paise_text = (fractional + "00")[:2] if dot else "00"
    return int(rupees) * 100 + int(paise_text)
```

Numeric spreadsheet cells need a separate, explicit path. Do not perform binary floating-point arithmetic. Convert a parsed numeric value through a decimal representation, validate that it has no more than two decimal places, and reject values whose source precision cannot be interpreted safely. Tests must cover cells represented as strings, integers and numeric spreadsheet cells. The parser above is a reference for text cells, not a complete spreadsheet-import implementation.

## 8. Audit trail and security posture

### 8.1 Minimum audit event

Each material event should include:

- Run ID and monotonically increasing sequence number.
- Event type and schema version.
- Timestamp in a declared format; generated at runtime.
- Operator identity reference from the actual local session/identity mechanism.
- Pseudonymous source-row identifiers, not unnecessary names or account values.
- Decision/action and relevant rule/configuration version.
- Previous event hash and current event hash.
- Input/output file hashes where appropriate.

Canonicalize event serialization before hashing, for example by a fixed JSON serialization policy, then compute the event hash from the prior hash and canonical current event. Provide a verifier that checks sequence continuity, hash links and expected source/output references.

### 8.2 Honest security claims

- A hash chain is **tamper-evident when verified against a trusted anchor**; it is not automatically immutable.
- A local log can be rewritten by an attacker with sufficient access unless a trusted checkpoint/signature is protected separately.
- A username written by the operator is not proof of identity. Production needs a defined authentication/role mechanism, or it must clearly state the level of assurance provided.
- Hashes do not prove that the underlying input data is true or correct.
- “Offline-capable” is the default product claim. Use “air-gapped” only when the actual deployment environment is physically or operationally isolated and this is demonstrated.
- Switching Wi-Fi off proves disconnected functionality, not the absence of all network behaviour. Network tests should separately document attempted outbound connections, telemetry, local loopback services and update behaviour.

### 8.3 Practical controls

- Read input files without modifying them; write outputs to a separate destination.
- Keep PII out of debug logs, crash reports and public screenshots.
- Use synthetic data for the hackathon.
- Use secure local storage and least-privilege filesystem permissions.
- Validate workbook formulas/exports and protect against formula injection from untrusted cell text.
- Package dependencies and model weights with a manifest, licence notices, version and cryptographic hashes.
- Updates must be signed or otherwise authenticated and integrity-checked; offline update packages need a documented process.
- Make a backup/recovery plan and test interrupted-write behaviour.

## 9. Technical architecture

### 9.1 Logical components

1. **Desktop UI:** file selection, schema mapping, progress, results, review queue and export.
2. **Import/normalization layer:** preserves raw values, detects header/date/amount formats and reports import issues.
3. **Deterministic reconciliation engine:** key matching, candidate generation, duplicate policy, one-to-one constraints, status assignment and amount arithmetic.
4. **Optional local-model adapter:** suggests unknown column mappings or drafts language from verified structured information. Its output is validated and never executed as arbitrary code.
5. **Review and authorization layer:** records approve/reject/field-inquiry decisions.
6. **Report generator:** writes the workbook and summary.
7. **Audit/provenance subsystem:** creates and verifies run/event hashes.

### 9.2 Model selection

**Primary candidate:** `Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` in a pinned GGUF quantization, initially evaluating Q4_K_M with `llama.cpp`.

The listed Qwen GGUF repository declares Apache-2.0 for this model distribution and currently lists the Q4_K_M file at approximately 1.12 GB. Pin the exact repository revision, file, digest and licence notice in the shipped software bill of materials. Do not use “0.98 GB” as a fixed specification without validating the exact artifact. Repository and file details: https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-GGUF

A small model is a candidate, not yet a proven fit. Benchmark its column-mapping accuracy and valid structured-output rate on the team's labelled examples. If it fails, use deterministic alias mappings and ask the user to map the column manually rather than accepting an unreliable suggestion.

**Secondary candidate:** Phi-3.5-mini or another commercially suitable model may be evaluated later. Do not bundle a secondary model until its exact licence, model card, redistribution terms, memory use and task quality have been checked.

### 9.3 Constrained output

Use a bounded schema and validated JSON/output parser where supported. Grammar-constrained decoding can enforce structure; it does not guarantee semantic truth. Validate all fields, enum values, length limits, paths and allowed actions after generation. Never let the model invent executable code or directly control file operations.

### 9.4 Memory and lifecycle

Do not promise a fixed RAM footprint before measuring it. Benchmark the full application, including OS baseline, office applications, model runtime, context/KV cache, spreadsheet import, fuzzy matching, UI and export. Prefer a single-model lifecycle: load the model only for tasks that need it, and release it when appropriate. Core reconciliation must still work if the model is not loaded.

## 10. Technical acceptance tests

The following are target tests, not claimed current results.

| Area | Test | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Correctness | Known exact matches, mismatches, unmatched records, duplicates and invalid rows | Golden dataset and machine-checkable expected results |
| Row conservation | Every imported row has exactly one primary status in each file | Invariant assertion succeeds for all test runs |
| Amount parsing | Whole rupees, paise, ₹ prefix, international/Indian comma styles, blanks, malformed values, negatives and excess precision | Unit-test report; invalid inputs are rejected and surfaced |
| Matching | Accents/transliteration variants, initials, swapped tokens, common names, same account suffix, amount mismatch and conflicting signals | Precision/recall and false-positive report against labels |
| Pair cardinality | Many-to-one and many-to-many candidate conflicts | No silent reuse of a row; conflicts enter review |
| Export | Totals, row identities, flags, formatting, formula-injection strings and source immutability | Output validation report and source hash comparison |
| Audit | Modify, remove or reorder an event; verify anchored chain | Verifier reports specific integrity failure |
| Performance | 500-row end-to-end run on a specified reference machine | Multiple timed runs; median and p95; clearly defined timing boundaries |
| Memory | Full run on 8 GB system under documented conditions | Peak resident memory and system-available memory log |
| Offline | Repeat workflow with external networking disconnected | Demonstration plus separate network observation/test report |
| Usability | Intended users complete a task without command line or prompt writing | Observed test notes, task completion and usability issues |

An under-1.5-second deterministic reconciliation time may be an aspirational benchmark for 500 rows on a named machine. Do not claim it until measured. Report file-load time, model-inference time, reconciliation time and export time separately, plus end-to-end time.

Do not split a requirement into smaller orders to avoid a procurement procedure.

The product's eventual classification as software, licence or service and the buyer's applicable rules need specific review.

### 12.3 Value proposition to test

The product sells a repeatable, guided and reviewable workflow—not a claim that Excel cannot do reconciliation. Test whether buyers value:

- Less repeated manual setup and formula maintenance.
- Clear, reviewable exceptions rather than hidden mismatches.
- Controlled human adjudication and consistent result formats.
- Reproducible calculations and a traceable audit history.

## 14. Market-sizing method

Do not reuse the prior ₹1,850 crore TAM or ₹4.2 crore SOM without a traceable calculation and source for each count.

Use these definitions:

- **TAM:** all plausible eligible workstations or office packs in the chosen market, multiplied by the relevant price; present assumptions and source data.
- **SAM:** the subset reachable given geography, buyer type, workflow need, procurement route, language/hardware support and product readiness.
- **SOM:** the subset the team could credibly reach and support over a defined period, based on capacity and likely procurement—not merely a percentage of TAM.

At ₹49,995 per five-seat pack, 285 offices with 15 seats each would imply 855 packs and a one-time gross licence value of ₹4,27,45,725 (approximately ₹4.27 crore). This is scenario arithmetic only. It does not prove that 285 offices exist in the target segment, that they have 15 eligible seats, or that they will buy.

A more concrete initial scenario is 25 new packs across Years 2 and 3, producing ₹12,49,875 of initial licence sales before maintenance revenue. Keep this scenario separate from market size.

## 15. Pilot and go-to-market plan

### Stage 1 — Hackathon prototype

Deliver an offline desktop demo, deterministic golden dataset, test report, known limitations and a crisp three-minute story. Only synthetic records are used.

### Stage 2 — University/incubator-supported validation

Seek a named faculty/incubator sponsor and an introduction to the relevant administrative stakeholder. Request a discussion and workflow observation first; do not assume an office will accept a pilot or share data.

### Stage 3 — Controlled pilot proposal

Before accessing operational data, obtain written authorisation and agree on: purpose, data fields, lawful basis/permissions as applicable, data minimisation, allowed devices, retention/deletion, access control, incident response, test plan, responsible staff, and whether the pilot is advisory only. Prefer synthetic or appropriately anonymised/de-identified data until the authority confirms what may be used.

### Stage 4 — Evidence-based pilot report

Report baseline and new task times; known-ground-truth match precision/recall; false-match cases; row-conservation results; financial totals; user task-completion rate; usability issues; system resource use; and security limitations. Do not claim “90% time saved” without measurement.

### Stage 5 — Procurement readiness

Confirm the applicable procurement channel and product classification with the prospective buyer. Prepare licence terms, support commitments, dependency/model licence inventory, software bill of materials, security test results, installation/update instructions and a costed quotation.

### Stage 6 — Repeatable expansion

Approach similar offices only after the first deployment is supportable and produces credible evidence of value. Track sales-cycle, deployment effort, support burden and renewals.

## 16. Legal, privacy and compliance posture

The product should be described as **designed for local processing and data minimisation**, not as automatically compliant with every law or departmental policy.

- Do not claim that DPDP Section 16 is an immediate blanket ban on all cloud computing.
- Do not state that offline execution alone guarantees DPDP compliance, CERT-In compliance or approval to process government records.
- Determine the applicable laws, commencement status, departmental instructions, data-handling obligations and procurement terms for the actual deployment date and data type.
- Confirm whether the department permits the proposed data to be processed on the intended workstation and by the proposed software.
- Keep model and dependency licences in the SBOM; check redistribution and notice obligations for exact versions.
- Do not claim an Aadhaar checksum establishes identity or that a locally run application is certified by UIDAI or any government authority.
- Obtain qualified legal/procurement review before commercial deployment or claims of formal compliance.

## 18. Risk register

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation / evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| False identity match from common names or masked suffix | Critical | Multiple signals, one-to-one constraints, labelled test set, mandatory review for fuzzy candidates |
| Amount mismatch hidden by matching policy | High | Separate linkage from financial comparison; explicit pair variance and unmatched totals |
| Duplicate rows cause double counting | High | Canonical duplicate policy, exactly one primary disposition per row, paired-file invariants |
| LLM makes poor header mapping | Medium | Alias dictionary first, schema validation, user confirmation, deterministic fallback |
| Incorrect arithmetic from malformed amounts | High | Strict parser, no silent rounding/coercion, integer paise, golden tests |
| Audit chain rewritten by privileged attacker | High | Trusted anchor/signed checkpoint and restricted storage; accurately describe limitations |
| Personal data leaks through logs/screenshots | High | Synthetic demo data, pseudonymous row IDs, log minimisation and access control |
| Offline claim is overstated | Medium | Distinguish disconnected operation from true air-gap; separate network-behaviour testing |
| Model/dependency licence issue | High | Exact version/revision licence audit, SBOM and notices before bundling |
| Unsupported procurement-threshold claim | High | Buyer-specific review; do not use price as a guarantee or split purchases |
| Low willingness to pay | High | Interviews, pricing tests, paid-pilot evidence and cost-to-serve analysis |
| Demo stalls on model output | Medium | Deterministic fallback; keep inference and note drafting outside the critical path |
| Scope creep | High | MVP exclusions, change log and phase gates |
