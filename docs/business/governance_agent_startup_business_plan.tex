\section{Executive Summary \& Three Levels of Scope}

\subsection{The Core Mission \& Operational Principle}
Public administrative offices (Tehsils, Block Development Offices / BDOs, and Municipal Wards) handle citizen welfare disbursements and records under tight operational deadlines, legacy computer hardware, and strict data confidentiality obligations.

Rather than promising an unconstrained general artificial intelligence that replaces human officers, the venture builds an \textbf{offline-first governance software platform} around a focused, demonstrable first product: the \textbf{Scheme Reconciliation Assistant}.

The central operational principle governing the entire product is:
\begin{quote}
    \textbf{“AI suggests. Deterministic code calculates. The officer decides. Every material action is traceable.”}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Separation of Scope Across Three Horizons}
To maintain complete credibility before government evaluators and hackathon juries, our capabilities are strictly partitioned into three distinct horizons:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Level 1: Strategic Vision (3 to 5 Years):} A local-first, privacy-conscious AI assistant for public administration. The long-term ambition is to assist authorized staff in processing fragmented administrative records into structured, auditable work products across multiple administrative workflows.
    \item \textbf{Level 2: Reusable Product Platform (6 to 24 Months):} A reusable desktop runtime incorporating a local inference adapter, deterministic execution tools, a human review/adjudication UI, and tamper-evident audit-chain infrastructure. Future modules (such as document extraction, circular search, archival retrieval, and administrative drafting) will be added only after independent testing.
    \item \textbf{Level 3: Locked V0 Prototype / First MVP (Innovathon 2.0 Focus):} The \textbf{Offline Scheme Reconciliation Assistant}. Ingests two spreadsheets (sanction list vs bank disbursement report), performs deterministic matching, flags exceptions, presents ambiguous candidates in a human review queue, and exports an audited Excel workbook. The MVP is designed for local execution without cloud inference; compatibility with 8 GB RAM workstations, end-to-end performance, and network-egress behaviour will be validated through documented tests before deployment claims are made.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem Hypothesis \& Competitive Differentiation}

\subsection{Problem Hypothesis to Validate with Users}
In grassroots offices across Jammu \& Kashmir and India, staff routinely compare records received from different sources: (1) Departmental Sanction Lists (beneficiaries, parentage, bank references, sanctioned amounts) and (2) Bank Disbursement / PFMS Reports (credits, UTR numbers, failure codes, disbursed amounts). 

\textbf{The Working Hypothesis:} Manual row-by-row reconciliation across large datasets creates a recurring clerical burden, increases the risk of undetected disbursement errors, and complicates audit defense when records do not reconcile cleanly. The team will validate the actual files involved, typical record volumes, baseline task times, and recurring error categories through structured interviews with administrative staff during the pilot discovery phase.

\subsection{Why Not Just Use Microsoft Excel or a Python Script?}
Judges and technical officers will rightfully ask why a packaged tool is needed when Excel or Python can perform lookups. Our differentiation is grounded in operational usability and process governance:

\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|l|Y|Y|Y|}
\hline
\textbf{Solution} & \textbf{What It Does Well} & \textbf{Operational Bottlenecks in Administration} & \textbf{Packaged Appliance Advantage} \\

& Flexible, ubiquitous, and familiar to office staff.

\subsection{Canonical Reconciliation Unit \& Cardinality Boundary (Locked V0 MVP)}
To establish exact mathematical determinism, the V0 prototype locks a concrete first-workflow reconciliation unit:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{File A (Departmental Sanction List):} Each row represents a \textbf{single beneficiary entitlement sanction for the declared payment cycle} (e.g., Monthly Welfare Pension Sanction Order, unique per beneficiary case).
    \item \textbf{File B (Bank / PFMS Disbursement Log):} Each row represents a \textbf{single beneficiary payment credit outcome for the same payment cycle} (e.g., PFMS Monthly Direct Benefit Transfer Report, unique per beneficiary account credit).
    \item \textbf{Locked Pair Cardinality:} Strictly \textbf{one-to-one (1:1)} in V0 MVP.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Payment / Split Transactions:} If an upstream bank file provides multiple transaction lines for a single entitlement (e.g., split credits ₹2,500 and ₹1,500 for a ₹4,000 sanction), V0's duplicate detection rule isolates the subsequent transaction against the same beneficiary to \code{DUPLICATE\_EXCLUDED} with the flag \code{MULTIPLE\_TRANSACTIONS\_DETECTED}. Multi-transaction aggregation (many-to-one ledger reconciliation) is documented as a Level 2 (reusable platform) roadmap module, explicitly excluded from the critical V0 demo.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Separating Record Linkage from Financial Reconciliation}
The engine strictly decouples two operational questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Record Linkage:} Do these two rows plausibly describe the same underlying beneficiary or case?
    \item \textbf{Financial Reconciliation:} Once linked, do the sanctioned amount and disbursed amount agree, and what is the exact variance?
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Critical Rule:} An amount mismatch does \textbf{not} disqualify a candidate match. If File A has \textit{“Mohd Iqbal, S/o Abdul Rashid, Acct ****-4812, Sanctioned ₹4,000”} and File B has \textit{“Mohammad Iqbal, S/o Ab. Rashid, Acct ****-4812, Disbursed ₹2,500”}, the system links them as a candidate pair and highlights the \textbf{₹1,500 Financial Discrepancy} for administrative review.

\subsection{Multi-Signal Candidate Matching \& Review Rules}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Signal Evaluation:} Candidate generation evaluates multiple corroborating identity signals (token/phonetic name similarity, parentage where present, masked account suffix where present, location identifiers).
    \item \textbf{Thresholds as Experiment Parameters:} A name similarity threshold (such as $0.82$) is an initial experiment parameter, not a locked business rule. Thresholds must be calibrated against labelled test datasets.
    \item \textbf{Missing vs. Conflicting Data:} Missing information does \textbf{not} count as agreement. Contradictory evidence (e.g., completely different parentage) is highlighted as a conflict.
    \item \textbf{No Auto-Approvals:} Every fuzzy candidate sits in \code{PENDING\_REVIEW} until an authorized human officer explicitly chooses an action: \textbf{[Approve as Match]}, \textbf{[Reject / Keep Separate]}, or \textbf{[Flag for Field Inquiry]}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Invariants \& Strict Integer Paise Parser}

\subsection{Mutually Exclusive Row Partitioning}
To guarantee zero record loss and mathematical consistency, every imported row in File A (Sanction List) and File B (Disbursement Report) is assigned \textbf{exactly one primary disposition}:
\begin{equation}
    N_A = M + U_A + D_A + I_A + P_A \qquad\text{and}\qquad N_B = M + U_B + D_B + I_B + P_B
\end{equation}
where $M$ is confirmed matched pairs (each containing exactly one row from File A and one from File B), $U$ is unmatched rows, $D$ is duplicate-excluded rows under the declared canonical duplicate policy, and $I$ is invalid/malformed rows. \textbf{$P_A$ and $P_B$ count distinct source rows assigned the primary status \code{PENDING\_REVIEW}, not candidate relationships.} Candidate edges are relationship metadata and do not inflate row counts, guaranteeing exact conservation. Secondary warning flags are tags and are never added to primary row counts.

\subsection{Strict Integer Paise Currency Parser}
To eliminate IEEE 754 floating-point representation drift (\code{5000.10} drifting to \code{5000.1000000000003}), all currency amounts are converted at ingestion into 64-bit integer paise:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
import re, math
from decimal import Decimal

_AMOUNT_RE = re.compile(
    r"^(?:[0-9]+|[0-9]{1,3}(?:,[0-9]{3})+|[0-9]{1,2}(?:,[0-9]{2})*,[0-9]{3})(?:[.][0-9]{1,2})?$"
)
_CURRENCY_PREFIX_RE = re.compile(r"^(?:₹|INR|Rs[.]?)\s*", re.IGNORECASE)

def parse_to_paise(amount_text: str) -> int:
    """Parse text into integer paise. Rejects negatives, malformed commas, excess decimals."""
    if not isinstance(amount_text, str):
        raise TypeError("Expected currency string; handle numeric cells explicitly")
    cleaned = re.sub(r"\s+", "", _CURRENCY_PREFIX_RE.sub("", amount_text.strip()))
    if not cleaned or not _AMOUNT_RE.fullmatch(cleaned):
        raise ValueError(f"Invalid currency amount: {amount_text!r}")
    normalized = cleaned.replace(",", "")
    rupees, dot, fractional = normalized.partition(".")
    paise_text = (fractional + "00")[:2] if dot else "00"
    return int(rupees) * 100 + int(paise_text)

def parse_cell_to_paise(val) -> int:
    """Parse cell (str, int, float, Decimal) into integer paise; rejects excess float precision."""
    if val is None or val == "": raise ValueError("Empty currency cell")
    if isinstance(val, bool): raise TypeError("Boolean not allowed")
    if isinstance(val, int):
        if val < 0: raise ValueError(f"Negative: {val}")
        return val * 100
    if isinstance(val, str): return parse_to_paise(val)
    if isinstance(val, float):
        if math.isnan(val) or math.isinf(val) or val < 0: raise ValueError(f"Invalid: {val}")
        d = Decimal(str(val))
    elif isinstance(val, Decimal):
        if val < 0: raise ValueError(f"Negative: {val}")
        d = val
    else: raise TypeError(f"Unsupported: {type(val)}")
    if d.as_tuple().exponent < -2: raise ValueError(f"Excess precision: {val}")
    return int((d * 100).to_integral_value())
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Financial Reconciliation Bridge}
The financial variance is calculated deterministically across verified categories:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Matched-Pair Variance } (\Delta_{\text{matched}}) = \sum_{i \in M} (S_i - D_i)
\end{equation}
Scheme-wide financial totals are reported across distinct accounting buckets: Total Sanctioned Amount ($\sum S$), Total Disbursed Amount ($\sum D$), Matched-Pair Net Discrepancy ($\Delta_{\text{matched}}$), Unmatched Sanctioned Total ($S_{\text{unmatched}}$), and Unmatched Disbursed Total ($D_{\text{unmatched}}$).

\section{Cybersecurity, Audit Trails \& On-Device Privacy}

\subsection{Tamper-Evident Hash-Chained Audit Trail}
Rather than claiming an impossible "immutable blockchain on a desktop," the application implements a \textbf{tamper-evident audit manifest} (\code{audit\_manifest.json}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Monotonically Increasing Sequences:} Every user action and processing step is logged with a sequential counter ($E_1, E_2, \dots, E_n$).
    \item \textbf{Cryptographic Binding:} Each event contains the SHA-256 hash of the preceding event:
    \begin{equation}
        H_k = \text{SHA-256}(H_{k-1} \,\|\, \text{CanonicalJSON}(E_k))
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Run Identity:} Records input file hashes, software build hash, local operator label, and execution timestamps generated at runtime.
    \item \textbf{The Trusted Anchor \& Verification Guarantees:} The terminal event hash $H_n$ and export manifest digest serve as the root anchor. When exported, this terminal hash can be printed on the official reconciliation summary sheet or logged to an external administrative register. A standalone verifier detects intermediate record alteration, reordering, or deletion; it cannot prevent a local administrator from regenerating an unanchored log from scratch.
    \item \textbf{Operator Identity Mechanism:} The prototype records a \textbf{local operator label} (e.g., workstation username or clerk session identifier); this is an operational audit tag, not cryptographic proof of user identity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{On-Device Data Minimization (DPDP Context)}
All processing occurs exclusively inside the RAM and local storage of the government workstation. Zero bytes leave the machine. We do not claim that running offline automatically grants "100\% statutory compliance" or that DPDP Section 16 is an active blanket ban on cloud services. Phased commencement of the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 was notified by the central government on 13 November 2025, with principal data-processing and fiduciary provisions scheduled to take effect 18 months post-notification. Applicable legal obligations must be reassessed for the actual pilot and deployment dates. We state that the software is architected to uphold the \textbf{data minimization and storage limitation principles} of the DPDP Act. Deployment remains subject to departmental policy, applicable law, and security review.

\section{Technical Acceptance Criteria, Known Limitations \& Risk Register}

\subsection{Technical Acceptance Criteria (Evidence-Based Gates)}
To replace unsubstantiated scorecards with engineering rigor, the prototype is evaluated against concrete acceptance gates:

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|l|Y|Y|}
\hline
\textbf{Dimension} & \textbf{Target Acceptance Criteria} & \textbf{Verification Method} \\
\hline
\textbf{Record Conservation} & Every source row has exactly one primary disposition; no silent record dropping. & Invariant check ($N = M + U + D + I + P$) verified by unit test. \\
\hline
\textbf{Financial Accuracy} & All calculations performed in integer paise; zero floating-point drift. & Parser test suite covering standard, comma-grouped, and malformed currency. \\
\hline
\textbf{Matching Quality} & Exact-match precision, candidate precision, recall, and false-match rates reported separately on synthetic benchmarks; synthetic results explicitly noted as not guaranteeing dirty field accuracy. & Labeled synthetic test dataset with known ground truth. \\
\hline
\textbf{Human Control} & Zero auto-approval of fuzzy candidates without explicit clerk confirmation. & Event log verifies operator label and explicit action before row is moved to \code{MATCHED}. \\
\hline
\textbf{Offline Operation} & Full workflow executes with networking physically and technically disabled. & Live demonstration with Wi-Fi disabled + external network traffic audit. \\
\hline
\textbf{Audit Verification} & Altered, reordered, or deleted log entries are detected by the verifier. & Integrity test script deliberately corrupting intermediate hash links. \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
